    Overview


        Research questions or hypotheses

Research Question: How do psychiatrists and LLMs differ in their conversations during psychiatric intake, and how do these differences influence clinical information gathering and interview quality?


        Foreknowledge of data or evidence

Data does not yet exist. No part of the data that will be used for this analysis plan exists, and no part will be generated until after this plan is registered.


        Explanation of foreknowledge and managing unintended influences

No data


    Research Design


        Study type

Simulation study: Using existing or synthetic data to assess performance of a model, mimic operation of an existing or proposed system, or predict possible outcomes in a controlled context. This may include demonstration of methods, many varieties of modeling, prediction of policy impacts, simulating behavior or outcomes in a population, and other designs.


        Intention for causal interpretation

Indirect inference on causal relationship(s): This study is intended to examine a potential causal relationship, but is not designed to isolate the specific causal effect. This work may inform causal inference indirectly or advance insight toward future estimation of a causal relationship. 


        Blinding of experimental treatments

No blinding is involved.


        Additional blinding during research or analysis

Coding will be conducted by independent raters who are blinded to interviewer type (clinician vs LLM). Transcripts will be anonymized by removing identifying information and standardizing formatting to minimize distinguishing features between human and LLM-generated text. Raters will code empathy dimensions (emotional validation, reflective listening, rapport building, patient-centered language) without knowledge of which condition produced the transcript. 

Quantitative analyses (information coverage, safety identification, question counts) rely on objective scoring against predefined ground truth and do not require blinding, as outcomes are determined by matching transcript content to established criteria rather than subjective interpretation


        Study design

We will be providing clinicians with links to our website (Github Hash: 4697f6d1142d69e8a352df279cdc4af89c829420) for data collection. They will first complete the consent form and fill out their name/affiliation. The experiment consists of one short in-app tutorial and one 20-minute simulated psychiatric intake session. The psychiatrist will receive 1 vignette at random from a pool of 3. They will see a brief vignette description, and will be given the full time to talk to the simulated patient. After the time limit, or if the clinician concludes the intake, they will be brought to a recall form screen. They will complete an open-response form about the intake, a likert-scale recall form, and a feedback survey.  

After all the clinician data, we will run LLM models on the same tool and vignettes. We will limit the LLM to the mean clinician interview duration. We will also consider doing other budgeted analysis on the LLM data, like a question-by-question analysis.

    Sampling


        Data collection procedures

Participants will be psychiatrists with experience conducting psychiatric intake. Participants will be recruited through professional networks, academic medical centers, and departmental listservs. Convenience sampling will be used given the specialized population.

Inclusion Criteria:
    Licensed or in-training mental health clinician
    Experience conducting psychiatric intake interviews
    Comfortable with text-based communication
    English fluency

Exclusion Criteria (Participation):
    No clinical experience with psychiatric interviewing

Exclusion Criteria (Analysis)
Sessions will be excluded from analysis if:
    Fewer than 5 interviewer messages sent
    Trap question failed (incorrect response to verifiable ground truth item)

Expected Sample Size
Target: 12 clinicians (pilot study)

Duration of Data Collection
Approximately 2-3 weeks

        Measured variables

Outcome Measures

Information Gathering
    Coverage accuracy: Proportion of ground truth items discussed in interview
    Precision accuracy: Proportion of recalled items that are correct (excluding "uncertain" responses)
    False confidence rate: Incorrect "yes" responses on undiscussed topics / total undiscussed topics
    Coverage by psychiatric domain (chief complaint, psychiatric history, family history, social history, substance use, functional impairment)

Safety Assessment
    Safety sensitivity: Identified concerns / total embedded concerns
    Safety specificity: Correct "no concern" / total cases without that concern
    Missed safety concerns (count)
    False positive safety identifications (count)

Recall Performance
    Overall recall accuracy
    Recall accuracy by psychiatric domain
    Recall completion time (seconds)

Question Formulation
    Total question count
    Open vs closed question ratio
    Follow-up question count
    Clarification question count
    Question density (questions per minute)

Engagement Variables
    Interview duration (seconds)
    Message count (total, interviewer only)
    Average interviewer message length (words)
    Follow-up question count

Variables for Exclusion Criteria
    Interviewer message count (exclude if less than 5)
    Trap question response (exclude if failed)

        Indices

Accuracy Indices

Coverage Accuracy
Formula: Correct responses / Total ground truth items
    Correct response: Recalled value matches ground truth
    Total ground truth items: Number of scorable fields in patient vignette

Precision Accuracy
Formula: Correct responses / (Correct responses + Incorrect responses)
    Excludes "uncertain" responses (Likert = 3)
    Measures accuracy when clinician commits to an answer

False Confidence Rate
Formula: Incorrect "yes" responses on undiscussed topics / Total undiscussed topics
    Undiscussed topic: Ground truth indicates information was not revealed in interview
    Incorrect "yes": Clinician indicates information was present when it was not discussed

Normalized Accuracy

Accuracy per 100 Patient Words
Formula: (Accuracy score / Patient word count) × 100
    Applied to coverage and precision scores
    Addresses potential confound of conversation length

Engagement Indices

Question Density
Formula: Total interviewer questions / Interview duration in minutes

Open Question Ratio
Formula: Open-ended questions / Total questions

Follow-up Ratio
Formula: Follow-up questions / Total questions

Safety Assessment Indices

Safety Sensitivity
Formula: Correctly identified safety concerns / Total embedded safety concerns

Safety Specificity
Formula: Correct "no concern" responses / Total cases without that specific concern

Recall Indices

Domain-Specific Recall Accuracy
Formula: Correct responses within domain / Total ground truth items within domain
Calculated separately for each psychiatric domain (chief complaint, psychiatric history, family history, social history, substance use, functional impairment)

Exclusion Indices
Trap Question Pass/Fail
    Binary: Correct response to embedded verifiable item = pass; incorrect = fail

    Original scale: 1 (Definitely No) to 5 (Definitely Yes)

        Inference criteria

Statistical Tests

All tests will be two-tailed unless otherwise specified.

Primary Comparisons 
    Mann-Whitney U tests for continuous outcomes
    Fisher's exact test for categorical outcomes (safety identification)
    Alpha level: p; 0.05
    Effect sizes reported for all comparisons:
        Rank-biserial correlation (r) for Mann-Whitney U
        Odds ratio with 95% CI for Fisher's exact

        Missing data

Item-Level Missing Data
    Recall form: All items are not optional, so form cannot be submitted with missing data. If the form is not completed, we will not use the data or have access to it. 
    Engagement variables: Calculated from available data; missing timestamps will result in exclusion of timing-dependent variables for that session only

Session-Level Missing Data
    If recall form not submitted, session excluded from recall analyses
    If interview terminated early but recall completed, session included with actual duration noted

Approach
    Complete case analysis will be used (no imputation)
    Sample sizes reported separately for each analysis to reflect any variation due to missing data
    Patterns of missingness will be examined and reported descriptively

LLM Condition
    No missing data expected for LLM interviews (fully automated)
    Any technical failures resulting in incomplete LLM transcripts will be regenerated

      Description

This pilot study compares the performance of human clinicians and large language models (LLMs) in conducting psychiatric intake interviews with simulated patients. Using a text-based interview platform, we examine differences in three domains: (1) clinical information gathering; (2) safety assessment; and (3) empathic communication patterns.

Clinicians will complete one 20-minute intake interview with a simulated patient presenting with psychiatric concerns. LLM interviews will be time-matched to mean clinician duration to enable fair comparison. Following each interview, participants complete a recall assessment measuring retention of clinical information. Interview transcripts will be analyzed for question types, information coverage, and safety concern identification. 

Primary outcomes include clinical information coverage, precision of recalled information, and safety concern detection rates. Exploratory analyses examine whether conversational engagement metrics (message count, follow-up questions) predict performance outcomes.
